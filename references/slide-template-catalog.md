# Slide Template Catalog (All 92 Types)

An image catalog generated by actually rendering each template registered under
`slide-templates/` one page at a time. Its purpose is to **let you look at the output and choose which template to use for a given slide**.
For how to write the schema, see [template-schema.md](template-schema.md);
for generic page patterns (skeletons and figure composition), see
[slide-pattern-catalog.md](slide-pattern-catalog.md).

Each template's **figures** line lists the `type` names of the drawing components used on that page.
Templates are used via `render_slide_template.py` or the `$template` field in a deck spec.

```bash
# Rebuild this catalog (same steps when adding a template)
for pack in marketing-analysis b2b-sales scalar-ae planning analysis read-alone business-plan nexus hearing case-studies proposal marketing partner; do
  .venv/bin/python scripts/build_slide_template_catalog.py \
      --pack $pack --out out/template-catalog/$pack.json
done
# Merge every spec's slides into one deck, generate with build_deck.py,
# then place the fetch_thumbnails.py PNGs at references/images/slide-templates/<id>.png
.venv/bin/python scripts/build_template_catalog_doc.py
```

> `architecture-exhibit`, `ui-mock-flow` and `ui-mock-detail` have no catalog
> image: they embed screenshots that only a real nexus-architect run produces.

## B2B Sales Pack (`b2b-sales`)

Pages that visualize a deal's stakeholder structure and discovery (issue exploration). Each page shares, on one slide, who influences the decision and what has not yet been learned.

### Influence Map (`influence-map`)

![Influence Map](images/slide-templates/influence-map.png)

Places buying-committee members on two axes — influence and stance — to show whom to move to win the deal

**Answers**: Whom do we need to move to close this deal, and is influence skewed toward the opposing side?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `posmap`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Diagnostic (identifying factors and structure)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- Base positions on statements confirmed in meetings. Do not place sales' own hopes as if they were the stakeholder's stance
- Do not place uncontacted stakeholders as "neutral." Put unknowns into discovery-gaps instead
- Base influence on whether the person actually mattered in past approvals, not on job title
- Keep bubble labels to 6 characters or fewer. Put job-title and name details in buying-committee instead
- Bubbles are 0.95in in diameter. If placed too close together, labels get hidden (the --dry-run figure audit stops on "Text is hidden behind a shape drawn later"). Merge stakeholders that appear at the same position, or split them out into buying-committee

### Buying Committee (`buying-committee`)

![Buying Committee](images/slide-templates/buying-committee.png)

Lists each buying-committee member's role, influence, stance, and contact status

**Answers**: Who is involved in the purchase, and how much access have we gained so far?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `table`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Descriptive (organizing facts)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- Write the buying role as the function this person serves in this deal (approver, champion, evaluator, user, gatekeeper, opponent), not their job title
- Write contact status based on actual meetings held. Do not count a mere business-card exchange as "contacted"

### Decision Structure (`decision-structure`)

![Decision Structure](images/slide-templates/decision-structure.png)

Shows, as a tree, which path an approval request follows and where it could stall

**Answers**: In what order does approval move up, and where might it stop?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `orgchart`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Descriptive (organizing facts)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- Draw this deal's approval route, not the org chart. Do not include departments that are not involved
- If the route changes depending on the amount, state in the source line that this is the route for the assumed amount
- Depth of 4 or more, or more than 8 leaves, causes text to become crushed. Split by department

### Discovery Map (`discovery-map`)

![Discovery Map](images/slide-templates/discovery-map.png)

Lists the items to confirm during the deal and color-codes each as confirmed, hypothesis, or unconfirmed

**Answers**: What do we know in this deal, and what is still just a hypothesis?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `ghost`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Diagnostic (identifying factors and structure)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- State is one of three values: confirmed (verified by the customer's statement or a document), wip (partially confirmed), or missing (unconfirmed)
- Do not mark sales' own guesses as confirmed. Anything whose corroborating source cannot be named is wip or lower
- Do not move on to drafting the proposal while red (missing) items remain

### Pain Chain (`pain-chain`)

![Pain Chain](images/slide-templates/pain-chain.png)

Traces how a frontline problem cascades into departmental and executive metrics

**Answers**: Which executive-level number is the frontline issue actually affecting?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `lead_in`, `flow`, `table`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Causal (asserting causes)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- A chain link is a causal claim. Do not connect two stages with an arrow unless each stage has supporting evidence
- Describe the impact on executive metrics using the customer's own numbers. If it's our own estimate, label it clearly as an estimate
- Do not present correlation as causation

### Unconfirmed Items and Next Steps (`discovery-gaps`)

![Unconfirmed Items and Next Steps](images/slide-templates/discovery-gaps.png)

Breaks down the open questions that remain blank into who will confirm them and by when

**Answers**: Whom do we need to ask what, next, to move the deal forward?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `lead_in`, `table`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Descriptive (organizing facts)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- Do not ask about an item if you cannot state why it matters. What decides success is the order of questions, not the quantity
- Do not leave a row without a person to confirm with and a deadline
- Keep this aligned one-to-one with the missing / wip items in discovery-map

### Influence Map (Org Structure) (`influence-map-org`)

![Influence Map (Org Structure)](images/slide-templates/influence-map-org.png)

Arranges buying-committee members by organizational connections, showing role, influence, stance, and meeting status

**Answers**: Who reports to whom, and where is influence and support concentrated?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `influence_graph`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Diagnostic (identifying factors and structure)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- The contents of people / links are validated by scripts/account_graph.py. Duplicate ids, dangling reportsTo references, and cycles are caught there, so always pass data that has gone through account_graph.load()
- If there are more than 9 people, thin them out with account_graph.extract() and write "see the draw.io version for the other N people" in more. Do not cram everyone onto one slide
- Include unmet stakeholders with met: false (drawn as a dashed line). Not having met someone is itself information
- Base stance on statements made in meetings. Do not write sales' own hopes into stance

### Discovery Map (Hierarchy) (`discovery-map-tree`)

![Discovery Map (Hierarchy)](images/slide-templates/discovery-map-tree.png)

Connects Goal, Strategy, and Tactics by support relationships, showing what supports what

**Answers**: What supports the customer's goal, and where do we make a difference?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `outcome_tree`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Strategic (evaluation and direction-setting)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- The contents of nodes / edges are validated by scripts/account_graph.py. Cycles and downward-pointing edges are caught there, so always pass data that has gone through account_graph.load()
- The tier is determined by graph depth, not by a tier field. A lower-level goal that supports a higher-level goal is placed one level below it
- If there are more than 9 nodes, thin them out with account_graph.extract() and write "see the draw.io version for the other N items" in more
- Do not place a goal in Goal that the customer never stated. Treat guesses as wip items in the discovery map instead

## Scalar AE Pack (`scalar-ae`)

Standard pages that Scalar account executives use for deal reviews and activity plans. Designed so the contents of account.json (the deal ledger) can be poured in directly. The only stable pack.

### Deal Snapshot (`account-snapshot`)

![Deal Snapshot](images/slide-templates/account-snapshot.png)

Shows, on one page, what stage this deal is in now, the amount, and when it will close

**Answers**: Where does this deal stand right now, for how much, and when will it close?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `cards`, `table`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Descriptive (organizing facts)  
**status**: stable

Guardrails:

- forecast is one of four values: Pipeline / Best / Commit / Closed. Demote a Commit to Best if you cannot state its basis
- Write an amount only if you can state how it was calculated. If you cannot, write "undetermined"
- Internal document. Do not share with customers or partners (it includes individuals' influence, stance, and internal politics)

### Stage-Gate Criteria (`phase-gate`)

![Stage-Gate Criteria](images/slide-templates/phase-gate.png)

Lists the current stage's exit criteria and judges each by whether customer-side evidence has been obtained

**Answers**: What customer-side evidence shows that the current stage's exit criteria are met?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `lead_in`, `table`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Diagnostic (identifying factors and structure)  
**status**: stable

Guardrails:

- State is one of three values: met / partial / unmet. "We explained it" or "we sent the proposal" does not count as grounds for met
- Evidence must come from the customer side (statements, documents, approval records). Internal agreement is not evidence
- Map each exit criterion to a gate ID in references/scalar/sales-playbook.md §2
- Internal document. Do not share with customers or partners (it includes individuals' influence, stance, and internal politics)

### BANT Risk (`bant-risk`)

![BANT Risk](images/slide-templates/bant-risk.png)

Identifies which of Budget / Authority / Needs / Timeframe is most likely to stall the deal

**Answers**: Where is the risk in this deal, and is the forecast reasonable?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `cards`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Diagnostic (identifying factors and structure)  
**status**: stable

Guardrails:

- Fix the order of the four items as Budget → Authority → Needs → Timeframe
- Open each item with ok / risk / unknown, and write the basis for that judgment in the same cell
- Send any item still marked unknown to action-plan with a person to confirm with and a deadline
- Internal document. Do not share with customers or partners (it includes individuals' influence, stance, and internal politics)

### Action Plan (`action-plan`)

![Action Plan](images/slide-templates/action-plan.png)

Breaks each unconfirmed issue down into who, asks whom, by when, and what completion looks like

**Answers**: To move this deal forward, who does what, by when?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `lead_in`, `table`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Strategic (evaluation and direction-setting)  
**status**: stable

Guardrails:

- Do not include a row whose completion criterion cannot be written. "Confirm it" is not a completion criterion
- Do not leave a row without a deadline. If the counterpart is internal, name the meeting body (WPS / Deal Desk)
- Map each row one-to-one to an unconfirmed item in discovery-gaps
- Internal document. Do not share with customers or partners (it includes individuals' influence, stance, and internal politics)

### Activity History (`activity-timeline`)

![Activity History](images/slide-templates/activity-timeline.png)

Lists, in chronological order, who we've met and what we learned

**Answers**: How much access have we gained with this customer, and what have we learned?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `timeline`, `table`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Descriptive (organizing facts)  
**status**: stable

Guardrails:

- Limit the table to the most recent 4 entries. Keep earlier history in the ledger (account.json); do not put it on the slide
- Write only the customer's own statements or documents under "what we learned." Do not write our own impressions
- Do not omit dates, so gaps where we had no contact remain visible
- Internal document. Do not share with customers or partners (it includes individuals' influence, stance, and internal politics)

### Visit Plan (`visit-plan`)

![Visit Plan](images/slide-templates/visit-plan.png)

Decides, before the visit, what to get out of it, how to ask, and how to respond

**Answers**: What do we want to get from the customer on this visit, and how will we respond to expected objections?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `lead_in`, `storyline`, `so_what`, `cards`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Strategic (evaluation and direction-setting)  
**status**: stable

Guardrails:

- Make the title "the one thing we want to hear from the customer." "Explain X" is not an objective
- Always state in ask which person or department we want introduced next, and why
- Frame questions so the answer isn't just Yes/No (include SPIN's Implication and Need-payoff questions)
- Internal document. Do not share with customers or partners (it includes individuals' influence, stance, and internal politics)

### Win Plan (WPS) (`win-plan`)

![Win Plan (WPS)](images/slide-templates/win-plan.png)

A one-page summary of the win strategy and risks used to decide stage advancement and proposal investment in WPS

**Answers**: Why can we win this deal, and how will we neutralize the biggest risk?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `exec_summary`, `table`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Strategic (evaluation and direction-setting)  
**status**: stable

Guardrails:

- Write in resolution why the customer would choose us, not a list of product names
- Pair each risk with a next action we can control. Anything without an actionable next step is capped at Best
- Always accompany this with a verbal request for a continue / hold / withdraw decision
- Internal document. Do not share with customers or partners (it includes individuals' influence, stance, and internal politics)

### Challenge Hypothesis One-Pager (`challenge-hypothesis`)

![Challenge Hypothesis One-Pager](images/slide-templates/challenge-hypothesis.png)

A one-pager that surfaces issues the customer hasn't yet articulated, to open a dialogue

**Answers**: What issue has the customer not yet noticed, and what happens if it's left unaddressed?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `lead_in`, `cards`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Diagnostic (identifying factors and structure)  
**status**: stable

Guardrails:

- **For customer presentation**. Do not write about individuals' influence, stance, internal politics, or competitors' weaknesses
- Fix the three cards in the order: groundwork → reframing → evidence (cost of inaction)
- Do not assert as fact. Phrase hypotheses so they read as hypotheses, and always end with a question to the customer
- Do not include figures without a source. Cite the source for any public case study

### License Estimate Summary (`license-estimate`)

![License Estimate Summary](images/slide-templates/license-estimate.png)

The cost page of a proposal: a Scalar license estimate's line items, list price → discount → offered price, and assumptions on one slide

**Answers**: How much does this configuration cost, and what are the breakdown and assumptions?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `lead_in`, `table`, `metric`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Descriptive (organizing facts)  
**densities**: print / presentation (default print)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- Transcribe unit prices and amounts from the price-master quotation; never compute them on this slide
- Never show the discount rate. Show only list total, discount amount, and offered price
- Always show the contract term (monthly / annual / 3-year) in the summary table; never state an amount without its term
- Pod counts must match the quotation and the architecture diagram. State the contract-Pod rule (annual contract = maximum concurrently running Pods) as an assumption

### License Pattern Comparison (`license-pattern-compare`)

![License Pattern Comparison](images/slide-templates/license-pattern-compare.png)

Compares quotation patterns (Standard / Premium, different configurations) side by side on configuration, Pod count, and offered price, with an explicit recommendation

**Answers**: Which license pattern fits this customer best, and how much do the options differ?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `lead_in`, `table`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Diagnostic (identifying factors and structure)  
**densities**: print / presentation (default print)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- Transcribe amounts from the price-master quotation's comparison summary; never show discount rates — compare offered prices
- Keep the row dimensions (configuration, Pods, term, price) aligned across patterns so each row reads straight across

## Planning Pack (`planning`)

Pages that present time-based plans. The three formats — Gantt chart, chronology, and milestone timeline — differ in granularity and use case.

### Gantt Chart (`gantt-schedule`)

![Gantt Chart](images/slide-templates/gantt-schedule.png)

A period-by-task bar chart showing work streams running in parallel and their milestones

**Answers**: When does what run in parallel, and when is the next milestone?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `gantt`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Descriptive (organizing facts)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- Start and end are decimals in column units (0 is the left edge of the first column, the column count is the right edge). Values exceeding the column count raise an error at generation time
- A row where start == end is drawn as a milestone (◆). Give a row width if you want it drawn as a bar
- Dependency arrows are not represented. If you need to show fine-grained sequencing, write it as a table instead
- Limit to 8 rows. Bundle fine-grained tasks so each row is one workstream
- Do not mix planned values with actuals. For a planned schedule, state the planning date in source

### Chronology (`chronology`)

![Chronology](images/slide-templates/chronology.png)

A vertical chronology of dates and events showing how we arrived at the current state

**Answers**: What has happened so far, and what sequence of events led to the current state?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `table`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Descriptive (organizing facts)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- Write only facts in the event column. Keep interpretation and evaluation separate, in "implications"
- Keep the date granularity consistent (all years, or all year-months, not mixed)
- Limit to 9 rows. Bundle earlier history or narrow the period covered
- If a quiet period is meaningful, don't omit the row — write "no change" to show it explicitly

### Milestone Timeline (`milestone-timeline`)

![Milestone Timeline](images/slide-templates/milestone-timeline.png)

Arranges milestones at equal intervals along a single horizontal timeline, showing the next decision point

**Answers**: How are the milestones arranged, and when is the next point we need to act?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `timeline`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Descriptive (organizing facts)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- Limit to 6 milestones. The timeline is drawn at equal intervals and does not represent duration (use gantt-schedule if you need to show duration)
- State in source that future milestones are planned, not confirmed
- Keep date labels to 10 characters or fewer (e.g., 2026/09), with consistent granularity

## Current-State Analysis Pack (`analysis`)

Pages built from consulting current-state analysis and issue-identification frameworks. Covers structural decomposition (logic tree / KPI tree), root cause (why-why / fishbone diagram), quantitative narrowing (Pareto chart), gap and process flow, external environment (PEST / Five Forces), and prioritization. Used by the current-state-analysis skill.

### Logic Tree (`logic-tree`)

![Logic Tree](images/slide-templates/logic-tree.png)

Decomposes an issue MECE-style, showing structurally where the problem (or the solution) lies

**Answers**: What elements can this problem be broken down into, and which one matters most?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `mece_tree`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Diagnostic (identifying factors and structure)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- tree is nested as [label, [children…]]. Each child is either the same shape or a string. Depth beyond 4, or insufficient column width, raises an error at render time
- Limit to 5 leaves (to preserve the 0.46in node height). Beyond that, split the tree across two slides
- MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive) is not enforced by the component itself. Keep one decomposition criterion per level
- Do not mix a Why tree (cause decomposition) and a How tree (solution decomposition) into a single tree
- Write in insight which branch matters most, and the basis for that judgment

### KPI Tree (`kpi-tree`)

![KPI Tree](images/slide-templates/kpi-tree.png)

Decomposes the KGI into its component metrics, showing which metric is driving the gap (or the achievement) against target

**Answers**: Which component metric is producing the gap between target and actual?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `mece_tree`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Diagnostic (identifying factors and structure)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- The root is a single KGI. Write node labels as "metric name\nactual value" (or "actual / target" if showing the ratio to target)
- Only use parent-child relationships explainable by arithmetic (Revenue = New + Existing, New = Leads × Conversion rate, …). Do not include elements that can't be explained this way
- Every figure requires a source. State the period and aggregation definition in source
- Limit to 5 leaves (to preserve the 0.46in node height for two-line labels). When there are many metrics, expand only the bottleneck branch and collapse the rest at a higher level
- Write in insight which metric is the primary driver of the gap, quantified (e.g., of the -12% shortfall, -9% comes from the opportunity-conversion rate)

### Why-Why Analysis (`why-why`)

![Why-Why Analysis](images/slide-templates/why-why.png)

Shows the chain produced by repeatedly asking "why" from an observed event down to the root cause

**Answers**: What is the root cause of this event, and where should we act?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `flow`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Causal (asserting causes)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- chain[0] is the observed event, and the last entry is the root cause. Don't skip steps — dig one "why" at a time
- Don't stop at "the person in charge was careless" or "low awareness." Dig down to systemic and process-level factors
- Word each step as something verifiable by fact. Mark any step that hasn't been verified with (hypothesis)
- Limit to 6 steps due to width constraints. If the cause branches into multiple paths partway through, use fishbone-diagram instead
- Write in insight the countermeasure (recurrence-prevention action) aimed at the root cause, distinguished from a symptomatic fix

### Fishbone Diagram (`fishbone-diagram`)

![Fishbone Diagram](images/slide-templates/fishbone-diagram.png)

Lists hypothesized causes of a problem by category, to identify where to start verifying

**Answers**: Which category does the cause of this problem fall under, and where should verification start?

**figures**: `governing_message`, `fishbone`, `so_what`, `source_note`  
**Inference level**: Diagnostic (identifying factors and structure)  
**status**: experimental

Guardrails:

- Keep categories to a single consistent basis, such as the 4Ms (Man, Machine, Method, Material) or process names. Do not mix bases
- Limit to 4 causes per category. If there are more, consolidate them or move them to a supplementary document (this diagram is not meant to prove exhaustive coverage)
